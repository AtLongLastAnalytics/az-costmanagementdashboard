# Executive summary

## The problem

Many businesses moved to the cloud because they were told it would be cheaper than running their own servers. Then the Azure bills started arriving, and nobody could explain them. The numbers changed every month, they couldn't be predicted, and they couldn't be justified to anyone else in the business.

This is not a rare situation. It is common to see a company that budgeted for a bill under $5,000 receive one closer to $30,000 — not because anyone did anything wrong, but because no one had ever built visibility into what was actually being spent. Cloud platforms are happy to charge you; they do not, by default, explain themselves in language a business owner understands.

## What this platform does

It turns an opaque monthly bill into something a business owner can read, predict, and explain. Specifically:

It **tracks every dollar** spent across all Azure services automatically, with no manual data gathering.

It **translates the jargon**. Instead of "Microsoft.Compute virtual machines — $340," you see "Servers — $340." That translation, applied across every line of the bill, is the difference between a report a business owner ignores and one they actually use.

It **warns you early**. You set spending thresholds that matter to you. The moment your spend crosses one, an email lands in the right inbox — automatically, the same day, not three weeks later when the invoice arrives.

It **shows you a clear picture**. A single dashboard breaks spending down by service, by area of the business, and over time, so you can see where the money is going at a glance.

It **explains what changed**. Every week, an automated report tells you what went up, what went down, and where costs are trending — so a surprise never builds up unnoticed.

## The outcome

The business owner moves from "I get a bill I can't explain" to "I know what I'm spending, I know why, and I'll hear about a problem before it becomes one." That is real-time financial control over cloud spend, delivered without anyone having to log in and check.

## Why it matters commercially

Cost control is one of the most valuable and least-served needs in cloud computing today. A platform that gives owners visibility and early warning protects them from exactly the kind of bill shock described above. For a consulting practice, it is a concrete, demonstrable offering: not "I know Azure," but "I built a system that gives business owners real-time visibility into their cloud spend and warns them before the bill becomes a problem."

It also runs cheaply by design. A tool whose entire purpose is to cut waste should not itself be wasteful — so the whole platform is built from lightweight, pay-only-for-what-you-use components.
